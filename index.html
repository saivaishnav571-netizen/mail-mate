        The pipeline

        Five passes over
        every message.

        No black box and no mailbox takeover. Each stage does one job, keeps its output visible, and hands the message to the next stage.

        Stage 01

        Read the whole message.

        MailMate normalises the sender, subject and body. It does not need access to your mailbox password, and it does not forward anything to a server.

        finance-budget-01.eml
        P
        Priya Raman priya.raman@northstar.example
        07:42

        Budget forecast: numbers due Friday

        Stage 02

        Sort it into one bucket.

        Keyword weights and sender domains produce a score. The winner gets the message; the score becomes a confidence number you can judge.

        Scored Budget forecast invoice · budget · finance · manager
        Important 7 Academic 4 Internship 4 Personal 4 Spam 5
        Filed as Important 92% confidence
        Stage 03

        Pull out what happens next.

        Dates near cues such as “by”, “before” and “due” become deadlines. Imperative sentences become actions. Both stay visible beside the message.

        “ The Q4 forecast needs your revised numbers by Friday 20 November at 5pm. Please upload the updated sheet to the finance drive before then.
        Deadline Fri 20 Nov · 5:00 pm
        Action Upload the updated sheet
        Reminder Thu 19 Nov · 5:00 pm
        Stage 04

        Write the reply, keep the pen.

        The draft uses the sender’s name, the action and the deadline. It is plain text, fully editable, and attached to the message until you decide otherwise.

        Re: Budget forecast: numbers due Friday Draft

        Hi Priya,

        Thanks for the details. I’ll upload the updated sheet to the finance drive by Fri 20 Nov, 5:00 pm.

        I’ll confirm once it’s done.

        Best,
        Alex

        Before you start

        Fair questions.

        The demo is deliberately small. Here is exactly what it does and does not do.

        Does MailMate connect to my real inbox?

        Not in this build. The demo uses a bundled 24-message inbox or a file you choose. There is no OAuth flow, no mailbox password field, and no server receiving your messages.

        What are the five buckets?

        Important, Academic, Internship, Personal and Spam. The order matters: Important is the default for messages with weak signals so a person can check them, while Spam needs a clear combination of giveaway phrases.

        How are deadlines and actions found?

        Dates are scored by nearby cues such as “by”, “before” and “due”, then the strongest candidate wins. Actions come from imperative sentences and requests such as “please upload” or “can you confirm”. Both are heuristics, visible for you to correct.

        Can it send email on its own?

        No. Every reply goes through an approval card with a required checkbox. The demo then simulates the send and records it on the page. No message leaves the browser, and there is no auto-send option.

        What happens when I upload a file?

        The file is read with the browser’s File API, validated row by row, then passed through the same sorting and drafting logic. Rows missing a sender, subject or body are listed as errors instead of being guessed at.

        Is the three-dimensional scene real?

        Yes. The sorting table is a live Three.js scene with envelopes moving into five paper trays. It pauses when you ask it to, when the tab is hidden, and when the scene is off screen. If WebGL is unavailable, a still paper fallback appears.
